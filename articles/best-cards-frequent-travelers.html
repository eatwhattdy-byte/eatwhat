What to Look for in a Card for Frequent Travelers

Card issuers market "travel cards" around perks — lounge access, points, sign-up bonuses. Those are worth having, but for anyone who travels internationally on a regular basis, the features that save the most money are quieter and rarely the headline on the ad.

The features that actually matter

No foreign transaction fee

This is the single biggest lever. A standard foreign transaction fee runs 1–3% of every purchase made abroad, which adds up fast for anyone spending regularly outside their home currency. A card with 0% foreign transaction fees effectively gives you the wholesale card-network exchange rate on every purchase — usually close to the true market rate.

No foreign ATM fee, or fee reimbursement

Separate from the transaction fee, many cards charge extra for withdrawing cash at a foreign ATM, and the ATM operator often adds its own fee on top. A card that reimburses ATM fees (even up to a monthly cap) removes one of the more consistent leaks in a frequent traveler's budget.

Wide acceptance network

A card with excellent rates that doesn't work reliably where you're going isn't useful. Coverage varies meaningfully by region — some networks are near-universal in Europe but spottier in parts of Asia, Africa, or Latin America. It's worth checking acceptance in your specific destinations, not just assuming a major network works everywhere.

Real-time transaction alerts

Card fraud detection systems sometimes flag legitimate foreign purchases as suspicious, especially early in a trip. A card with an app that sends instant alerts (and lets you confirm or dispute in real time) avoids the frustration of a declined card at a checkout counter in an unfamiliar city.

Features that sound good but matter less for this purpose

  • Airport lounge access — genuinely nice, but irrelevant to what you pay when converting currency.
  • Large sign-up bonuses — a one-time perk, not a reflection of ongoing value for regular international spending.
  • Premium metal card design — has no bearing on exchange rates or fees.
A simple test: before choosing a card for travel, check its foreign transaction fee, its foreign ATM fee policy, and its acceptance in your usual destinations. If those three are strong, the rest is a bonus rather than a deciding factor.

Debit vs. credit for travel

Credit cards with no foreign transaction fee are generally the better default for purchases, since they add a layer of fraud protection and don't touch your cash directly. A fee-free debit card (or one that reimburses ATM fees) is usually the better choice specifically for withdrawing local cash, since ATM rates tend to be close to the market rate.
